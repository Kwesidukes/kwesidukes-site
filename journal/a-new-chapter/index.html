A New Chapter,
8,000 Miles Away.

Sometimes the biggest change isn't the distance you travel. It's the life you decide to build when you arrive.

By Kwesi Dukes · Published 11 November 2026

On May 3, 2026, I arrived in Australia. There is a simple way to describe that moment: I moved from the United States to Perth. But a change like that does not fit neatly into a single sentence. A new address is one thing. A new chapter of life is something else entirely.

For years, my work in technology taught me how to adapt. Systems change. Priorities shift. You learn to solve the problem in front of you, make decisions with imperfect information, and keep moving. Starting over in another country asks something different of you. It asks what you really want your life to be about when there is no familiar routine to lean on.

There are practical parts to beginning again, of course. New surroundings. New processes. A different rhythm to everyday life. There is work to find, work to do, and a future to plan. But behind all of that is a quieter question: What kind of life am I trying to build?

I know I want freedom. Not freedom as an abstract idea or something to talk about someday. I mean the ability to make choices without every decision being governed by necessity. The freedom to build a home, travel, enjoy a good meal, spend time with the people I love, and help my children when I can. The freedom to do meaningful work without losing sight of the life that work is supposed to support.

A new chapter isn't a promise that everything will be easy. It's a decision that the next part of the story is worth building.

I have spent a lot of my life working through challenges, learning new skills, and showing up where I'm needed. Those things still matter to me. I take pride in doing work well. But I am learning to measure progress by more than a title, a project, or a number. I want the things I build to mean something outside of work, too.

That is part of why this Journal exists. I don't want every experience to disappear into a calendar, a photograph, or a passing conversation. I want somewhere to reflect on what I am learning, what I am creating, and who I am becoming along the way. Some entries will be about technology. Others will be about music, family, ambition, or the ordinary days that end up meaning more than we expected.

Perth is where this chapter begins. I don't know everything it will hold, and I don't need to pretend that I do. What I do know is that I came here to build a life, not just a résumé. This is my record of that journey.
